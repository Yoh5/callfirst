Courier scams end at the front door. So does this.

The scam starts on the phone: a grandchild in trouble, a federal agent, the bank. It ends when a courier comes to collect the cash or the gold. The person at home is expecting the courier, so alerting on strangers does not help.

How it works

  1. Someone unexpected rings. Not a visit or a delivery declared in advance. The chime plays the reminder: the one code the scammer cannot know is the family's.
  2. The door is opened for them. The family is told at once, with the doorbell's still image and how sure the system is: a door sensor saw it, or only how long the visitor stayed.
  3. The courier comes back. They often do. For a week, the family is told as soon as anyone unexpected appears, before the door opens.
  4. Someone talks the resident into switching it off. The family is told that too. Scammers demand secrecy; this breaks it.

The false alerts and the misses are published with the results: a neighbour let in unannounced is a false alert, and without a door sensor a handover under a minute is missed.

What it will not do

  • Recognise faces. Or anyone. It never tries to identify a visitor.
  • Call the police. False alerts exist; people decide.
  • Let a model decide. A model may read a pasted delivery message. Code checks it against the message itself.
  • Run without the resident's say. The resident switches it on, chooses who is told, and can delete everything in one step.
