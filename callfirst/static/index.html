Call First

Courier scams end at the front door. So does this.

The scam starts on the phone — a grandchild in trouble, a federal agent, a bank — and ends when a courier comes to collect cash or gold. The FBI counted at least 1,737 of these pickups, about $186 million, between 2023 and May 2025. The person at home is expecting the courier, so alerting on strangers does not help.

What it does, on the Ring doorbell already there

  1. Someone unexpected rings — not a visit or a delivery declared in advance — and the chime says: “Before you hand anything over, call your family first, and ask for your family word.”
  2. The door is opened for them, and the family is told at once, with the doorbell's still image and how sure the system is.
  3. The courier comes back — they often do — and for a week the family is told as soon as anyone unexpected appears.
  4. Someone talks the resident into switching it off, and the family is told that too. Scammers demand secrecy; this breaks it.

What it will not do

  • Recognise faces, or anyone.
  • Call the police. People decide.
  • Let a language model decide anything. A model may read a pasted delivery message; code checks it against the message itself.
  • Run without the resident's say. The resident switches it on, chooses who is told, and can delete everything in one step.
